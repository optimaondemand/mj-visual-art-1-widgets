LESSON 13
Ancient China: The Pagoda Landscape
Instructional Content · Week 1: Learn & Look
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
A pagoda is a tall tower built from one shape stacked again and again, set inside a wide landscape. How do artists use shape and space to make a flat drawing feel tall, deep, and far away?
This week you will look closely at a pagoda, a tiered tower with many roofs, and at the way artists place near things and far things to build space. You will learn to see two of an artist's quietest, most powerful tools at work: shape and space.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what a pagoda is and where the form came from.
2
I will be able to find shape and space at work in a real picture of a pagoda landscape.
3
I will be able to plan a pagoda landscape drawing that stacks shapes into a tower and uses near and far placement to create space.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

Before we name anything, let's look.

This is a hand-colored print from 1814 A.D. It shows a tall pagoda, a tiered tower, standing on a white bridge over a long, straight canal. People in old-fashioned clothes crowd both banks. Spend a full minute looking before you read on.

🧠 SEE · NOTICE · WONDER

👀 What do you SEE? A tall, thin tower in the middle. It is built from many rings stacked on top of one another, getting smaller as they go up, with a small point at the very top. It sits on a white bridge over a long stretch of water. Tents, trees, flags, boats, crowds of people, and even a balloon in the sky fill the rest of the scene.

🔍 What do you NOTICE? The tower is made of the same basic shape repeated, just shrinking as it rises. Things in front (boats, people) look big; things far away (trees, the far bank) look small and sit higher up. That difference is what makes the picture feel deep.

💭 What do you WONDER? Why stack shapes into such a tall, narrow tower? And how does an artist make a flat sheet of paper feel like it stretches far into the distance? By the end of this lesson you will know.

📚 PART 2 · NAME WHAT YOU SEE

Pagodas, Chinese landscape, and the tools of shape and space

A pagoda is a tall, tiered tower with several roofs, one above another. Each level has a wide roof, called an eave, that often curves up at the edges. The pagoda form grew out of Buddhist architecture in Asia: early pagodas were built to hold sacred objects and to mark holy places. Builders stacked floor on floor, each a little smaller than the one below, so the tower seems to climb toward the sky.

Pagodas often appear in Chinese landscape art, a long tradition of painting mountains, water, trees, and small buildings. Chinese landscape artists are masters of space. They rarely show one flat wall of scenery. Instead they overlap shapes and place near things low and large, far things high and small, so a calm, deep world opens up on the page.

Now meet the two art tools this module is built around. Watch for each one in the pagoda picture.

📐
SHAPE — a flat, closed area with an edge all the way around, like a circle, square, triangle, or rectangle. In the pagoda: the tower is built by stacking shapes, a tall rectangle for the body, rows of roof shapes between the floors, and a small triangle and circle at the very top.
🌋
SPACE — the feeling of depth or distance in a flat artwork: what is near, what is far, and the room around things. Artists build space by overlapping (one shape in front of another) and by near/far placement (near things low and large, far things higher and smaller). In the picture: the boats in front are large and low, the far trees are small and high, so the canal feels long and deep.
A quick look at SHAPE and SPACE — the pagoda's simple shapes (squares and roof triangles) are stacked into a tall tower, and space comes from how the parts are placed one above another.

🔎 PART 3 · WATCH IT WORK

How an artist reads the pagoda landscape

Artists look in a careful order: first they describe, then they analyze, then they interpret, and only then do they judge. Watch how it works on the pagoda print.

DESCRIBE — what is there? A tall, tiered pagoda stands on a white bridge over a long canal. Tents, trees, flags, boats, and crowds fill the banks, and a balloon floats in the sky.
ANALYZE — how is it built? The tower is made of stacked shapes that shrink as they rise. The artist creates space by placing the boats and crowds large and low in front, the trees and far bank small and high behind, and by letting the bridge overlap the scenery.
INTERPRET — what might it mean? The tall pagoda becomes the calm center of a busy, happy scene, drawing the eye upward while the deep canal opens the view far into the distance.
JUDGE — does it succeed? Most viewers say yes: the stacked shapes give the tower height, and the near/far placement gives the flat print a real sense of deep space.

📚 VOCABULARY
Pagoda
A tall, tiered tower with several roofs, one above another.
Eave
The wide, often up-curved edge of a roof on each floor of a pagoda.
Tier
One level or floor in a stack of levels.
Finial
The small ornament, often a point or a knob, at the very top of a tower.
Shape
A flat, closed area with an edge all the way around (circle, square, triangle).
Space
The feeling of depth and distance in a flat artwork: near, far, and the room around things.
Overlapping
Placing one shape in front of another so the hidden one looks farther away.
Horizon line
The line where the land or water seems to meet the sky.
Emphasis
A Principle of Design: the part of an artwork the eye is pulled to first, the focal point.
Buddhist
Relating to Buddhism, the religion whose temples gave rise to the pagoda form.
