The Taj Mahal: Pattern, Balance, and Space
Instructional Content · Week 1: Learn & Look
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
Why does a building so perfectly even feel so calm and grand? How do artists use pattern, balance, and space to make us feel that way?
Almost 400 years ago, builders in India raised a white marble tomb so carefully balanced that its left side is a mirror of its right. It is called the Taj Mahal. This week you will learn to see three powerful design tools at work in it: pattern, balance, and space.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to define pattern, balance (bilateral symmetry), and space.
2
I will be able to find each of these three at work in the Taj Mahal.
3
I will be able to plan and build a symmetrical cardboard relief that uses pattern, balance, and space.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

Before we name anything, let's look.

This is the Taj Mahal in the city of Agra, India. A Mughal emperor named Shah Jahan had it built between 1632 and 1648 A.D. as a tomb, called a mausoleum, for his wife Mumtaz Mahal. It is made of white marble. Spend a full minute looking before you read on.

🧠 SEE · NOTICE · WONDER

👀 What do you SEE? A white marble building with a tall, rounded dome in the middle and a deep, pointed archway under it. Four slender towers stand at the corners. A long, narrow pool runs straight toward you with the building reflected in the water, and rows of dark green trees line the path.

🔍 What do you NOTICE? The left side and the right side look exactly the same, like a mirror. Cover one half with your hand and the other half tells you what is hidden. The long pool pulls your eye straight to the center. As the pool and trees stretch back toward the building, they seem to shrink into the distance.

💭 What do you WONDER? Why does a building this even and this still feel so peaceful? By the end of this lesson you will be able to answer that, using pattern, balance, and space.

📚 PART 2 · NAME WHAT YOU SEE

Three design tools: Pattern, Balance, Space

Last modules you met the Elements of Art. Pattern and balance are Principles of Design, the rules artists use to arrange the elements. Space is an Element of Art. Watch for all three inside the Taj Mahal.

◠◡
PATTERN — a design element that repeats over and over. In the Taj Mahal: the marble surfaces are covered in repeating pointed arches, flowers, and lines of writing set into the stone, called inlay work. The same shapes appear again and again.
⚖️
BALANCE — an even spread of visual weight so a design feels steady. The Taj Mahal uses bilateral symmetry: the left half is a mirror image of the right half across a center line. In the Taj Mahal: the central dome sits on the middle line, and the four minarets are placed evenly, two on each side.
🗺️
SPACE — the area around, between, and behind things, including the illusion of depth. In the Taj Mahal: the long reflecting pool and the rows of trees grow smaller as they reach back toward the building, which makes the flat photo feel deep, as if you could walk into it.
A quick look at BILATERAL SYMMETRY — both halves match across a center line. Look at the Taj Mahal head-on: the left side is a mirror of the right.

🔎 PART 3 · WATCH IT WORK

How an artist reads a building

Artists look in a careful order: first they describe, then they analyze, then they interpret, and only then do they judge. Watch how it works on the Taj Mahal.

DESCRIBE — what is there? A white marble building with a tall rounded dome, a deep central arch, and four corner towers, set behind a long reflecting pool lined with trees.
ANALYZE — how is it built? Bilateral balance makes the two halves match; repeating pattern covers the marble; the receding pool builds deep space. The design blends Persian, Islamic, and Indian traditions.
INTERPRET — what might it mean? The calm, even design can feel peaceful and dignified, a fitting memorial built in someone's honor.
JUDGE — does it succeed? Most viewers say yes: nearly 400 years later, the Taj Mahal is admired around the world as one of the most balanced buildings ever made.

The three tools you just met — pattern, balance, and space — are the same three you will put to work yourself.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI & ART
An AI image tool can render a perfect, symmetrical Taj Mahal in seconds. It cannot give you the understanding of why bilateral symmetry feels balanced, or the steadiness in your own hand as you mirror one half onto the other. That understanding is yours to build, and this week's drawing is how you build it.
📚 VOCABULARY
Pattern
A design element that repeats over and over.
Balance
An even spread of visual weight so a design feels steady.
Bilateral Symmetry
When both halves of a design mirror each other across a center line.
Space
The area around and between things; near vs. far.
Mausoleum
A large, grand building made as a tomb.
Minaret
A tall, slender tower on an Islamic building.
Dome
A rounded roof; the Taj Mahal's is an "onion" dome that tapers to a point.
Inlay Work
Setting cut pieces of stone into a surface to form patterns.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Pattern repeats, balance steadies, and space gives depth. The Taj Mahal uses all three so well that it has felt calm and grand for almost 400 years. Learn to see these tools, and you can use them in your own work.
