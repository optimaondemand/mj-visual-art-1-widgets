📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
This year you made art inspired by cultures all over the world. If you could open your own museum of that work, what would you choose to show, and how would you arrange it so a visitor understood your story?
All year you traveled through world art: Japan, Egypt, Greece, Rome, Africa, India, China, Australia, Native America, the Maya, and the Aztec. Now the project changes. You will not make a new artwork this time. Instead, you will become a curator and build a museum out of the art you already made.

📚 PART 2 · WHAT A CURATOR DOES

The four jobs of a curator

A curator is the person at a museum who decides what art the public gets to see and how. A museum is not just a pile of artworks in a room. Someone chose each piece, decided where it would hang, and wrote the words beside it. That someone is the curator. The curator has four main jobs.

✅
1. SELECT — A curator chooses which works to show. A museum cannot display everything, so the curator picks the pieces that are strongest or that best tell a story together. Choosing what not to show matters as much as choosing what to show.
🗂️
2. GROUP — A curator puts works into groups, called an exhibition or a gallery. Works might be grouped by where they came from (all the Egyptian art together), by an Element of Art (everything bold with line), or by a theme (art about animals).
🧭
3. ARRANGE — A curator decides the order works are seen in. The arrangement guides a viewer, like a path. A strong first piece pulls people in. Placing two works side by side invites visitors to compare them.
🏷️
4. LABEL — A curator writes the small cards, called labels, beside each work. A good label gives just enough to help a viewer look more closely, without telling them what to think.
The parts of a museum label — for this museum, a good label has three parts:
1. TITLE
What you call the work, like "My Egyptian Cat."
2. CULTURE / TRADITION
The world culture that inspired it, like Ancient Egypt or Edo-period Japan.
3. ONE SENTENCE
One sentence about the work, what it shows or how you made it.

How arrangement guides a viewer

The order of works tells a story even before anyone reads a label. A curator might arrange a museum in different ways, and each choice sends a different message:

• By culture — group all the works from one part of the world together, so a visitor takes a trip around the globe.
• By Element of Art — put all the bold-line works in one place and all the colorful ones in another, so a visitor sees how one tool can be used many ways.
• By personal favorite — lead with your strongest piece so visitors are pulled in right away, then build toward another high point at the end.

There is no single right order. The curator decides, and then makes sure the arrangement matches the story they want to tell.

🔎 PART 3 · A CURATOR READS THE WALL

How a curator decides where Hokusai's wave goes

A curator looks at a work in a careful order before placing it: first describe, then analyze, then interpret, and only then judge where it should hang. Watch how it works on The Great Wave.

DESCRIBE — what is there? A huge wave curling over three small boats, with Mount Fuji small and calm in the distance, all in deep blue and white.
ANALYZE — how is it built? Strong line shapes the curling foam; space makes the near wave huge and the far mountain tiny; the blue and white color ties it all together.
INTERPRET — what might it mean? The wave feels powerful and a little dangerous, while the mountain feels steady, as if nature is both wild and calm at once.
JUDGE — where should it go? It is bold and instantly recognizable, so a curator might hang it near the entrance as a strong first work that pulls visitors in. The arrangement is itself a choice the curator makes.
💡 YOUR TURN IS COMING

Over the next weeks you will become the curator of your own World Art Museum. You will look back at the art you made all year, choose five or six works, decide how to group and arrange them, write a label for each, and add a Curator's Statement that ties your museum together. The same four jobs you just learned, select, group, arrange, and label, are the ones you will do yourself.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI & ART
An AI tool can sort photos of your artwork into folders or suggest an order in seconds. It cannot decide which of your works is worth showing, or say why a piece belongs in your museum. Choosing what to show and explaining why is the curator's judgment. That judgment is yours.
📚 VOCABULARY
Curator
The person who selects, groups, arranges, and labels the art in a museum.
Museum
A place where art is kept, cared for, and shown to the public.
Gallery
A room or space in a museum where artworks are displayed.
Exhibition
A group of artworks chosen and arranged to be seen together.
Label
The small card beside a work with its title, culture, and a sentence about it.
Curator's Statement
A short note from the curator about what the whole exhibition shows.
Arrangement
The order and placement of works, which guides how a viewer moves through.
Portfolio
Your saved collection of the artwork you made across the year.
💼 CAREER CONNECTION
The job you are practicing in this capstone is a real one. A few people who work in museums:
Museum curator: chooses and arranges the artworks in an exhibition, and researches each one.
Exhibit designer: plans how a gallery looks and flows, so visitors move through it well.
Conservator: cares for and repairs artworks so they last.
Docent: leads tours and talks with visitors about the art.
