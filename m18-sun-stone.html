LESSON 18
The Aztec Sun Stone: Radial Balance
Instructional Content · Week 1: Learn & Look
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
Some artworks do not have a left side and a right side. They have a center, and everything spins out from it. How does a design that radiates from the middle hold together?
The Aztec people carved one of the most famous round stones in the world. Its whole design starts at a single face in the center and grows outward in rings. That way of arranging a picture has a name: radial balance. This lesson teaches you to see it, and the carved symbols that fill it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to define radial balance and explain how a design radiates from a center.
2
I will be able to explain what a symbol is and find symbols carved into the Sun Stone.
3
I will be able to plan my own round design that radiates evenly from a center.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

Before we name anything, let's look.

This is the Aztec Sun Stone, also called the Calendar Stone (in Spanish, Piedra del Sol). It is a huge round carving made from a single block of dark volcanic stone called basalt. The Mexica people, whom we usually call the Aztecs, carved it in their capital city of Tenochtitlan, on the site of present-day Mexico City. It dates to the late Postclassic period, around the early 1500s A.D. Spend a full minute looking before you read on.

🧠 SEE · NOTICE · WONDER

👀 What do you SEE? A large round stone, carved all over. Right in the middle is a face. Around that face are several rings, like circles inside circles, each one packed with small carved shapes and symbols.

🔍 What do you NOTICE? The design starts at the center face and grows outward, ring after ring, all the way to the edge. Inside each ring the shapes repeat at even spaces, so the carving looks balanced no matter which way you turn it.

💭 What do you WONDER? Why would an artist build a picture out from a center instead of left to right? By the end of this lesson, you will know that this arrangement is called radial balance, and you will plan your own.

📚 PART 2 · NAME WHAT YOU SEE

Radial balance and symbols

Two ideas do most of the work in the Sun Stone. The first is a Principle of Design called radial balance. The second is an idea about meaning called the symbol.

☉
RADIAL BALANCE — a design where everything is arranged around a center point and seems to spread outward, like the spokes of a wheel or the rays of the sun. In the Sun Stone: the carving begins at the central face and grows ring by ring out to the edge.
⬥
CENTER & CONCENTRIC RINGS — concentric rings are circles that share the same center, one inside the next. In the Sun Stone: ring after ring of carved symbols circles the central face, each ring inside the one before it.
📝
SYMBOL — a picture or shape that stands for an idea, not just for itself. A heart can stand for love; a dove can stand for peace. In the Sun Stone: the small carved shapes are symbols. Many of them stand for days, time, and Aztec beliefs about the world.
📐
PATTERN — a design element repeated again and again. In the Sun Stone: the symbols inside each ring repeat at even spaces, which is what makes the radial balance feel steady.
🌝
SUN POINTS — around the outer edge, carved points and rays reach outward. They give the whole stone the feeling of a sun shining in every direction, which is one reason it is called the Sun Stone.
A quick look at RADIAL BALANCE — everything on the Sun Stone is arranged evenly in rings around one center point.

🔎 PART 3 · WATCH IT WORK

How an artist reads the Sun Stone

Artists look in a careful order: first they describe, then they analyze, then they interpret, and only then do they judge. Watch how it works on the Sun Stone.

DESCRIBE — what is there? A large round stone with a face in the center, circled by several rings of small carved symbols, and pointed rays around the outer edge.
ANALYZE — how is it built? The design uses radial balance, spreading out from a center; the rings are concentric; each ring repeats symbols in a steady pattern.
INTERPRET — what might it mean? The Aztecs placed important meaning at the center and built the world outward from it. Scholars still debate who the central figure is. It is often identified as the sun deity Tonatiuh, and sometimes as the earth deity Tlaltecuhtli, so we say honestly that experts do not fully agree.
JUDGE — does it succeed? Many viewers say yes: even five centuries later, the design pulls your eye straight to the center and holds together evenly all the way around.
