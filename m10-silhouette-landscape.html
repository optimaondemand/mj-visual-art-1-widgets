LESSON 10
Value, Contrast, and the Silhouette Landscape
Instructional Content · Week 1: Learn & Look
📖 ESSENTIAL QUESTION
A single black shape against a glowing sky can be one of the strongest pictures you will ever make. Why does putting the darkest dark next to the lightest light work so well?
Picture the African savanna at sunset: a flat-topped acacia tree and a few animals stand pure black against a sky of orange, gold, and pink. That picture is built from two tools, value (how light or dark something is) and contrast (the difference between light and dark). This week you will learn to control both.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain value and contrast and read a value scale from light to dark.
2
I will be able to explain why a silhouette read against a graded sky shows high contrast at its strongest.
3
I will be able to plan a sunset silhouette landscape that uses value to blend a sky and contrast to make a focal point.
👀 PART 1 · LOOK FIRST

Before we name anything, let's look.

This is a photograph of the African savanna at sunset. A few flat-topped acacia trees stand almost pure black against a sky glowing orange, gold, and red, with the sun sinking low behind their branches. Spend a full minute looking before you read on.

🧠 SEE · NOTICE · WONDER

👀 What do you SEE? Flat-topped trees standing pure black along the bottom, and a wide sky above them glowing yellow and orange near the sun, turning red and then dark toward the edges.

🔍 What do you NOTICE? The trees have no color or detail inside, just crisp black shapes. They stand out most sharply where they cross the brightest part of the sky, and the sky itself blends smoothly from light to dark.

💭 What do you WONDER? How can a plain black shape, with nothing drawn inside it, still make such a strong picture? By the end of this lesson you will be able to answer that, using value and contrast.

What makes a picture like this so strong? Two tools. Value is how light or dark something is, and every color and grey can be sorted from lightest to darkest on a value scale:

Now look back at the photograph. The trees sit at the dark end of that scale; the glowing sky sits at the light end. When the darkest dark meets the lightest light, you get high contrast. A dark shape with no inner detail, like those trees, is called a silhouette.

📚 PART 2 · NAME WHAT YOU SEE

Value and contrast, and the silhouette they make

Here are the ideas you will use this week. Watch how each one points toward the sunset savanna picture.

◑
VALUE — how light or dark something is. Every color also has a value. In the savanna: the sky near the setting sun is high value (light), and it gets lower (darker) as you look up.
▤
VALUE SCALE — values lined up in order from lightest to darkest. In the savanna: a sunset sky is a value scale you can see, blending from light at the horizon to darker overhead.
◐
CONTRAST — the difference between values placed side by side. A small difference is low contrast; a big difference is high contrast. In the savanna: black tree against bright sky is about as high as contrast gets.
🌲
SILHOUETTE — a solid dark shape with no inner detail, read only by its outline. In the savanna: the acacia tree is a silhouette. You know it by its edge, not by any bark or leaves inside.
💡
FOCAL POINT — the spot your eye goes first. High contrast creates one. In the savanna: the bright sun, or a dark tree sitting against the lightest part of the sky, pulls your eye in.

Put it together: a silhouette is the simplest way to show value and contrast at their strongest. Because the shape is one flat dark value, all the work is done by the sharp edge where it meets the bright sky.

🔎 PART 3 · WATCH IT WORK

How an artist reads a sunset silhouette

Artists look in a careful order: first they describe, then they analyze, then they interpret, and only then do they judge. Here is that order used on the sunset photograph above, the kind of scene you will make.

DESCRIBE — what is there? A glowing orange-and-gold sky fills most of the picture, brightest around the low sun. Along the bottom, flat-topped acacia trees stand out in solid black.
ANALYZE — how is it built? The sky is a value gradient, light at the horizon and darker above. The black shapes are silhouettes. The dark-against-light meeting is high contrast, and the bright sun becomes the focal point.
INTERPRET — what might it mean? The quiet, the end of the day, the wide openness of the land. The black shapes feel calm and still against the glow.
JUDGE — does it succeed? It works when the darks are truly dark, the lights are truly light, and the edges stay crisp. Muddy middle values in the wrong places would weaken it.

⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI & ART
An AI image tool can blend a perfect gradient sky in a single click. What it cannot do for you is mix value by hand and judge where dark should meet light to make the contrast strongest. That eye-training is the whole point of this project, and that judgment is yours.
📚 VOCABULARY
Value
How light or dark something is.
Value scale
Values lined up from lightest to darkest.
Contrast
The difference between light and dark placed side by side.
High contrast
A big value difference; darkest dark next to lightest light.
Silhouette
A solid dark shape with no inner detail, read by its outline.
Gradient
A smooth blend from one value or color to another.
Focal point
The spot the eye goes first; high contrast creates one.
Horizon line
The line where the land or sea meets the sky.

💪 THE BIG IDEA

Value is how light or dark something is. Contrast is the difference you create when you place them side by side. A silhouette against a glowing sky is the strongest, simplest way to use both at once: darkest dark, lightest light, one crisp edge between them.
